Overview

Jira managed the tasks. The creative process between them was still fragmented.

01 / Challenge

The workflow lived outside the tickets.

Smaller checkpoints, stakeholder approvals, ownership and stage progression often existed across conversations, email, meetings and project files.

02 / Opportunity

Make the workflow itself visible.

I explored a structured system connecting stages, tasks, ownership, time, evidence and approvals—without trying to replace the team's existing project-management tools.

03 / Outcome

From one branding flow to a configurable system.

Across 18 iterations, the concept evolved into reusable stage templates. The functional prototype was tested with designers and reviewed with Design leadership and Operations.

01 / Problem

The problem wasn't managing tasks. It was managing what happened between them.

Creative projects already had tasks, owners, deadlines and Jira tickets. But completing a ticket didn't necessarily mean the project was ready to progress. The process depended on smaller checkpoints, reviews, stakeholder decisions and approvals that weren't always represented in the workflow.

Problem reframed

A task can be complete while the project still isn't ready to move forward.

That distinction changed the direction of the product. The missing layer wasn't another way to create tasks—it was a way to represent project progression, dependencies and decision points around those tasks.

02 / Product strategy

Don't replace Jira. Design the layer around it.

I deliberately positioned the concept as a workflow layer rather than another general project-management product. The central question became: what needs to be true before this project should move forward?

03 / Design principles

Three principles shaped the system.

01

Structure without rigidity

Create a repeatable process while allowing teams to adapt stages and move incomplete work when real projects don't follow the ideal path.

02

Progress requires evidence

Major transitions should be tied to explicit approvals and deliverables—not assumptions that a conversation meant “approved.”

03

Make state visible

Someone entering a project should quickly understand where it stands, what needs attention, who owns the work and what prevents progression.

04 / System architecture

From a branding SOP to a configurable workflow model.

The first concept followed the branding process I already used. Through iteration, I separated the underlying workflow mechanics from the content of any one discipline—making stages reusable and editable for different teams.
